# Risks, failure modes and learning features of LLM-maintained personal knowledge bases (LLM Wiki pattern)

Research date: 2026-10-12. Evidence-quality tags used below: **[PR]** peer-reviewed, **[PP]** preprint / conference paper, **[PRAC]** first-hand practitioner report (anecdote), **[SEC]** secondary summary of others' comments, **[VEND]** vendor blog with a product to sell, **[DOC]** tool documentation, **[NOTE]** expert working notes (not peer-reviewed).

## Q1. Failure modes of LLM-maintained wikis and the safeguards practitioners use

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed study of the LLM Wiki pattern itself exists. Practitioner reports (April–September 2026) agree on a few failure modes. Errors compound when generated answers are filed back as pages. Claims go stale. Whole-page rewrites drift. Index, duplicate and link quality drops past roughly 150–200 pages. Lint tends to be skipped. The safeguards that keep coming up are: raw sources kept immutable, git with diff review, scoped edits limited to named pages, a dated source pointer per claim, explicit supersession instead of silent overwrites, and periodic lint. Practitioners also note that the lint model shares the blind spots of the model that wrote the error. A peer-reviewed ACL 2025 study confirms that repeated LLM reprocessing of the same text causes progressive drift, which grows with chain length.

### Cited Findings

**The original pattern (what it does and does not cover)**
- Karpathy's gist "llm-wiki.md" was created 4 April 2026. It defines three layers: immutable raw sources ("This is your source of truth"), an LLM-owned wiki ("You read it; the LLM writes it"), and a schema file (CLAUDE.md/AGENTS.md). It also defines three operations: ingest, query and lint [DOC/PRAC]. — [Karpathy gist](https://gist.github.com/karpathy/442a6bf555914893e9891c11519de94f)
- One ingest may touch 10–15 pages. Good query answers can be "filed back into the wiki as new pages". This file-back loop is what critics blame for compounding errors. — [Karpathy gist](https://gist.github.com/karpathy/442a6bf555914893e9891c11519de94f)
- Lint in the gist checks for contradictions between pages, stale claims superseded by newer sources, orphan pages, concepts mentioned without their own page, missing cross-references, and data gaps a web search could fill. — [Karpathy gist](https://gist.github.com/karpathy/442a6bf555914893e9891c11519de94f)
- Karpathy prefers to "ingest sources one at a time and stay involved" and browses edits live in Obsidian. The wiki "is just a git repo of markdown files". The index.md approach "works surprisingly well at moderate scale (~100 sources, ~hundreds of pages)". The gist has no risks section and does not discuss error propagation. — [Karpathy gist](https://gist.github.com/karpathy/442a6bf555914893e9891c11519de94f)

**Error compounding / hallucinations becoming "facts"**
- A summary of all 1,061 comments on the gist (17 Aug 2026) quotes a 12-month production user: "a bad wiki article becomes a prior that poisons future generations". In multi-agent setups, agents cite each other's unverified entries [SEC]. — [Omid Sayfun, "I read all 1,061 comments…"](https://omiid.me/notebook/66/i-read-all-1-061-comments-on-karpathy-s-llm-wiki-gist)
- Critics describe the loop like this: a page with a subtle mistake feeds an answer, the answer is filed back, and "now two pages reinforce the same error". The polished format (clean markdown, cross-references, citations) also makes readers more likely to trust incorrect content [SEC; from search snippet, page not fetched]. — [Denser.ai, "LLM Wiki: Karpathy's Idea…"](https://denser.ai/blog/llm-wiki-karpathy-knowledge-base/)
- The lint pass is weak protection because "the linter is the same kind of model that made the error". LLM-compiled wikis have no built-in way to trace a claim to an exact source location, and generated citations can themselves be wrong [VEND, Proudfrog, 8 Apr 2026]. — [Proudfrog, "The LLM Wiki at Scale"](https://proudfrog.com/en/insights/llm-wiki-skeptics-guide)
- One essay argues that "Write-time synthesis is a loan against future correctness". It also says the KB "doesn't degrade in a detectable way. It drifts", becoming "a closed epistemic loop that cites itself". It concedes the pattern suits one user, modest scale and active review [opinion; from search snippet, not fetched]. — [foundanand, "The Hidden Flaw in Karpathy's LLM Wiki" (Medium)](https://foundanand.medium.com/the-hidden-flaw-in-karpathys-llm-wiki-e3a86a94b459)

**Drift and homogenisation after repeated rewrites**
- **[PR, ACL 2025]** Mohamed, Geng, Vazirgiannis & Shang, "LLM as a Broken Telephone". When an LLM's output becomes the input of the next iteration (translation chains in their experiments), "small alterations in phrasing, meaning, or factual details can accumulate, leading to a progressive drift from the original source". Longer chains degrade more. Distortion "is unavoidable" but can be mitigated by lower temperature and restrictive prompting. Caveat: the experiments use translation chains, so applying them to summary rewrites is an extrapolation. — [arXiv 2502.20258](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.20258v2); [ACL Anthology](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2025.acl-long.371)
- A first-hand user running the pattern daily for three months reports: "Codex sometimes rewrites entire pages when only a paragraph needed updating… This causes wiki drift over time". The same user gets "more predictable diffs from Claude Code" [PRAC, Kunal Ganglani, 15 Apr 2026, updated 16 Sep 2026]. — [Ganglani, "LLM Wiki Setup"](https://www.kunalganglani.com/blog/llm-wiki-karpathy-local-knowledge-base)
- Ganglani also reports style and naming drift across pages. He recommends a style guide (naming conventions, length targets, page templates) in the system prompt and normalising the wiki every 20–30 sources [PRAC]. — [Ganglani](https://www.kunalganglani.com/blog/llm-wiki-karpathy-local-knowledge-base)
- Homogenisation in human output (indirect evidence): people who learned via LLM summaries wrote advice that was shorter, cited fewer facts and "showed greater similarity to other participants' advice" [PR, PNAS Nexus 2025, n=10,462]. — [Melumad & Yun, via PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/learning-from-ai-summaries-leads-to-shallower-knowledge-than-web-search/); [Stanford SCALE repository](https://scale.stanford.edu/ai/repository/experimental-evidence-effects-large-language-models-versus-web-search-depth-learning). The "Your Brain on ChatGPT" study also reports "homogenization of linguistic output" among LLM users [PP]. — [Architecture & Governance commentary](https://www.architectureandgovernance.com/applications-technology/cognitive-debt-the-logical-extension-of-cognitive-offloading/)

**Stale claims, silent overwrites, index drift, orphan pages**
- Notes "rot against reality". Example: a note said a dashboard required admin access, but the permission check returned `true` for everyone. Other failures listed: silent overwrites, index drift, orphan pages and numbers with no source pointer [SEC]. — [Sayfun](https://omiid.me/notebook/66/i-read-all-1-061-comments-on-karpathy-s-llm-wiki-gist)
- A vendor post lists typical DIY failures: the raw folder "stops growing after the first two weeks", and "the lint pass gets skipped until the wiki becomes inconsistent enough to be unreliable". The result: "The entries look authoritative. They are increasingly wrong." [VEND, remio, April 2026; based on second-hand gist comments, not on usage data]. — [remio](https://www.remio.ai/post/karpathy-s-llm-wiki-pattern-has-16-million-views-here-s-what-it-looks-like-actually-running)

**Scale, context limits, duplicates, over-linking**
- "When your wiki grows past roughly 150-200 pages, most agents can't hold the full wiki in context during an update. The agent starts 'forgetting' pages exist and creates duplicates or misses cross-links." This is the author's rough observation, not a measurement [PRAC]. — [Ganglani](https://www.kunalganglani.com/blog/llm-wiki-karpathy-local-knowledge-base)
- Ganglani's linking rules: link only the first mention of an entity per page, and never link to pages that don't exist [PRAC]. — [Ganglani](https://www.kunalganglani.com/blog/llm-wiki-karpathy-local-knowledge-base)
- Extra context hurts agents. The comment summary cites Chroma's measurement of 18 frontier models degrading as input grew, and Drew Breunig's finding that a small model failed a benchmark with 46 tools but passed with 19. Recommendation: "scope, don't filter", using separate corpora per domain [SEC]. — [Sayfun](https://omiid.me/notebook/66/i-read-all-1-061-comments-on-karpathy-s-llm-wiki-gist)
- One commenter claims the approach collapses past ~1,000 files [SEC; from search snippet, not verified]. — [Denser.ai](https://denser.ai/blog/llm-wiki-karpathy-knowledge-base/)
- Token cost is an author estimate, not a measurement: about 35k tokens per ingest (10–15 pages × ~3k tokens + prompt), about 300k tokens for a full lint of a 100-article wiki, and $5–50/month at Opus-tier pricing. The post itself calls these "back-of-envelope" figures [VEND]. — [Proudfrog](https://proudfrog.com/en/insights/llm-wiki-skeptics-guide)

**Safeguards reported by practitioners**
- **Immutable raw sources plus git.** Karpathy keeps raw sources read-only and the wiki in git. Proudfrog recommends treating the compiled wiki as regenerable from immutable, git-versioned raw sources. — [Karpathy gist](https://gist.github.com/karpathy/442a6bf555914893e9891c11519de94f); [Proudfrog](https://proudfrog.com/en/insights/llm-wiki-skeptics-guide)
- **Diff review and scoped edits.** Review `git diff` after every update. "Don't ask the agent to update the whole wiki"; name the pages to change. Have the agent list the pages a new source would touch before it edits anything [PRAC]. — [Ganglani](https://www.kunalganglani.com/blog/llm-wiki-karpathy-local-knowledge-base)
- **Human promotion gate.** Agents draft freely, a human promotes, and "unpromoted drafts don't count as evidence" [SEC]. — [Sayfun](https://omiid.me/notebook/66/i-read-all-1-061-comments-on-karpathy-s-llm-wiki-gist)
- **Dated pointers.** Claims about external systems link to what they describe and carry a check date. Lint re-verifies the oldest ones first [SEC]. — [Sayfun](https://omiid.me/notebook/66/i-read-all-1-061-comments-on-karpathy-s-llm-wiki-gist)
- **Supersession callouts instead of silent edits**, a word cap on the map/index file, and a scheduled lint every other day (Sayfun's own KB) [PRAC]. — [Sayfun](https://omiid.me/notebook/66/i-read-all-1-061-comments-on-karpathy-s-llm-wiki-gist)
- **Freshness field.** A `last_updated` frontmatter field to find stale pages, plus a weekly contradiction-check prompt that cites sources [PRAC]. — [Ganglani](https://www.kunalganglani.com/blog/llm-wiki-karpathy-local-knowledge-base)
- **Random claim audits.** Audit five random claims weekly for the first month, tracing each to its source. Define in advance the quit metrics (contamination rate, cost, maintenance hours). Keep AI-generated content in a separate vault (attributed to Steph Ango) [VEND]. — [Proudfrog](https://proudfrog.com/en/insights/llm-wiki-skeptics-guide)
- **Split lint.** A community "karpathy-llm-wiki" skill splits lint into deterministic auto-fixes (index consistency, broken internal links, raw references) and heuristic reports (contradictions, orphans, stale archives) [community code]. — [skillselion listing](https://skillselion.com/skills/astro-han/karpathy-llm-wiki)
- **Contradiction flagging at ingest.** When a new source arrives, the LLM compares it with existing pages and flags conflicts, "leaving the judgment call to the human" [blog]. — [yu-wenhao, Karpathy vs Zettelkasten](https://yu-wenhao.com/en/blog/karpathy-zettelkasten-comparison/)
- **Confidence scoring and supersession.** "LLM Wiki v2" (rohitg00) reportedly adds confidence scoring, supersession of stale claims and contradiction detection, after append-only ingest broke past a few hundred sources [SEC; from search snippet, repo not fetched]. — [theaioperator, "Karpathy's LLM Wiki Gist Decoded"](https://theaioperator.io/p/i-rebuilt-karpathys-llm-wiki-heres)

### Inferences
- ms-kb already has several of the reported safeguards: a git commit per write, refusal when the file changed meanwhile, duplicate/similar-title checks, and an append-only `extend`. Allowing a topic page to be rewritten (the user's decision) brings in exactly the "whole-page rewrite drift" and "loss of correct details during consolidation" failure modes. The Broken Telephone result suggests drift grows with every rewrite cycle. Cheap mitigations follow from the sources: show a diff of the rewritten page before saving, have the agent list removed claims explicitly ("dropped: …"), and keep rewrites scoped to named sections.
- ms-kb has no raw-sources layer. Pages are written from conversations, not from immutable documents, so the "re-derive from raw" safeguard is unavailable. Git history is the only ground truth. A "verify against primary docs" pass then needs URLs on the claims. The dated-pointer pattern (URL plus check date) fits this better than per-claim "assumption" callouts.
- File-back of query answers is the main amplification loop in reports. The user's flow (research, then a consolidated summary) is a file-back loop by design. The risk is highest when an agent consults the KB and then writes the result back without re-checking sources.
- A personal vault with hundreds of topic pages will reach the reported 150–200-page context ceiling. Duplicate detection in the CLI (deterministic), rather than in the model, is the right place for the safeguard.

### Gaps
- No controlled or quantitative study of LLM-maintained wikis was found. Error rates, contamination rates and drift per rewrite are unmeasured. All failure reports are anecdotal or second-hand from comment threads.
- No source measured "loss of correct details during consolidation" in personal notes specifically. Summarisation faithfulness research (omissions) exists but was not covered here.
- The "~1,000 files" collapse and the "150–200 pages" ceiling are unverified individual claims.
- The rohitg00 "LLM Wiki v2" design (confidence scoring, supersession) was not read directly.

## Q2. Marking uncertainty or verification status without cluttering notes

### Takeaway
Practitioners use three families of markers. (1) A page-level status or confidence label: Gwern-style belief tags, epistemic-status lines, growth stages with "planted / last tended" dates. (2) Per-claim provenance as a dated pointer: a URL plus a check date. (3) Explicit supersession callouts for changed claims. No empirical evidence was found on which approach works best for personal notes. The rationalist community's experience suggests a small, fixed vocabulary is more useful than freeform statuses.

### Cited Findings
- The epistemic-status line originated with muflax, passed to Gwern, then to Scott Alexander. Gwern formalised it with a variant of intelligence-estimate words (Kent's "words of estimative probability" scale) plus a "log" label for pages that document rather than predict [SEC]. — [EA Forum explainer (GreaterWrong)](https://ea.greaterwrong.com/posts/bbtvDJtb6YwwWtJm7/); [Issa Rice, belief.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/riceissa/issarice.com/master/wiki/belief.md)
- Issa Rice's wiki uses a graded verbal scale (certain, highly likely, likely, possible, unlikely, highly unlikely, remote, impossible) and does not display the tag where belief doesn't apply. — [Issa Rice, belief.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/riceissa/issarice.com/master/wiki/belief.md)
- Critique of freeform statuses: standard words were meant to let readers "quickly and easily share and compare the confidence"; freeform epistemic statuses "are the opposite of that". — [GreaterWrong comment on "Feature idea: epistemic status"](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/Hrm59GdN2yDPWbtrd/feature-idea-epistemic-status/comment/CYdktQRoBXp2xM3FQ)
- Maggie Appleton's "Epistemic disclosure" argues that stating effort and confidence upfront sets reader expectations, because disappointment comes from "unmet expectations". Her own pages show "Planted" and "Last tended" dates and a growth stage ("evergreen"). Gwern is described as attaching a "certainty" rating to each post, e.g. "highly likely" [blog; undated]. — [Maggie Appleton](https://maggieappleton.com/epistemic-disclosure)
- Dated pointers (claim → link + check date, oldest re-verified first) and supersession callouts instead of silent edits [SEC]. — [Sayfun](https://omiid.me/notebook/66/i-read-all-1-061-comments-on-karpathy-s-llm-wiki-gist)
- `last_updated` in YAML frontmatter for freshness tracking [PRAC]. — [Ganglani](https://www.kunalganglani.com/blog/llm-wiki-karpathy-local-knowledge-base)
- Human promotion gate as a status: drafts vs promoted pages, where drafts "don't count as evidence" [SEC]. — [Sayfun](https://omiid.me/notebook/66/i-read-all-1-061-comments-on-karpathy-s-llm-wiki-gist)
- Related learning-science caution: repeated studying "increased students' confidence in their ability to remember" without better delayed recall (Roediger & Karpicke 2006) [PR]. Polished text inflates felt certainty. — [Roediger & Karpicke 2006 PDF](https://psychology.ecu.edu/wp-content/pv-uploads/sites/216/2019/03/Roediger-Karpicke-2006.pdf)

### Inferences
- For ms-kb, the sources point to a combination. Use one page-level frontmatter property (e.g. `status: draft | checked` or a small fixed confidence vocabulary). Keep one consolidated "To verify" list at the end of the page for open claims, not inline callouts per claim. Use footnote-style or end-of-section source links with a check date for claims that were verified. This matches the dated-pointer and fixed-vocabulary patterns and removes the noise the current "assumption" callouts create.
- A per-claim callout carries information only if most claims are verified. When most claims come from the model's background knowledge, marking every one is noise. Inverting the default fits that case better: mark verified claims with a source and treat unmarked ones as unverified.
- Supersession (old claim struck through or moved to a "Changed" note with date and reason) keeps rewrites auditable without keeping per-day history. This matches the user's "no per-day learning history" decision while git keeps the full trail.

### Gaps
- No empirical study was found comparing confidence markers, footnote citations or status properties in personal notes, or their effect on later trust or recall. All evidence is convention and opinion.
- Gwern's current scale was not verified on gwern.net itself. Sources disagree on whether it is numeric or verbal.

## Q3. Learning science: generation, retrieval, spacing, elaboration, interleaving, and what LLM-era studies show

### Takeaway
Classic evidence is strong and consistent. Practice testing and distributed practice are the highest-utility techniques (Dunlosky et al. 2013). Retrieval beats restudy on delayed tests (testing effect g ≈ 0.50–0.61; larger with feedback). Self-generated material is remembered better (d ≈ 0.40). Elaborative interrogation, self-explanation and interleaving are moderate-utility. Rereading, highlighting and summarisation are low-utility. 2024–2025 studies on LLMs show the same split. Unguarded AI help raises in-task performance but lowers independent performance or depth: −17% on an exam without AI (PNAS 2025), shallower knowledge than web search (PNAS Nexus 2025), and less effort with weaker reasoning (CHB 2024). Tool designs that force the learner to do the work largely avoid the harm. An AI-written wiki is therefore best used as material for the user's own retrieval and explanation, not as reading material.

### Cited Findings

**Classic techniques**
- **[PR] Dunlosky, Rawson, Marsh, Nathan & Willingham (2013)**, Psychological Science in the Public Interest 14(1):4–58. High utility: practice testing and distributed practice. Moderate: elaborative interrogation, self-explanation, interleaved practice. Low: summarization, highlighting/underlining, keyword mnemonic, imagery for text, rereading. — [APS press release](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2013/01/130110111734.htm)
- **[PR] Roediger & Karpicke (2006)**, Psychological Science. Prose passages, either tested (free recall, no feedback) or restudied. Restudy was better on an immediate test, but "prior testing produced substantially greater retention than studying" at 2 days and 1 week. Repeated studying inflated confidence. (A secondary summary gives 61% vs 40% at one week; not verified in the primary text.) — [Roediger & Karpicke 2006 PDF](https://psychology.ecu.edu/wp-content/pv-uploads/sites/216/2019/03/Roediger-Karpicke-2006.pdf); [WUSTL profile](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/test-enhanced-learning-taking-memory-tests-improves-long-term-ret/)
- **[PR] Rowland (2014)**, Psychological Bulletin meta-analysis: 159 effect sizes from 61 studies; testing vs restudy g = 0.50. With feedback g = 0.73, without g = 0.39. Larger effects for retention intervals ≥ 1 day, and recall-type initial tests beat recognition. Lab studies only. — [Rowland 2014 PDF](https://courseware.epfl.ch/assets/courseware/v1/fdde2f0aa590bf3b1324077a6bf1540c/asset-v1%3AEPFL%2BDEMO%2B2020%2Btype%40asset%2Bblock/Rowland2014-meta-analysis.pdf)
- **[PR] Adesope, Trevisan & Sundararajan (2017)**, Review of Educational Research 87(3): 272 effect sizes; practice tests vs other conditions g = 0.61. Benefits held for retention and transfer. Mixed-format tests were most effective. (Article count reported as 118 or 188 in different summaries.) — [WSU PDF](https://education.wsu.edu/documents/2018/01/rethinking-use-tests.pdf/); [Learning Scientists summary](https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2017/2/9-1)
- **[PR] Bertsch, Pesta, Wiscott & McDaniel (2007)**, generation-effect meta-analysis: 445 effect sizes over 86 studies, mean effect ≈ 0.40 SD in favour of generated over read material. Larger with longer retention intervals and when less material has to be generated. — [PubMed 17645161](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17645161/)
- **[PR] Cepeda, Pashler, Vul, Wixted & Rohrer (2006)**, Psychological Bulletin 132(3): 839 assessments in 317 experiments. The optimal gap between study sessions grows with the desired retention interval. — [PubMed 16719566](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16719566/); [full PDF](https://www.evullab.org/pdf/CepedaPashlerVulWixtedRohrer-PB-2006.pdf)
- **[PR] Brunmair & Richter (2019)**, "Similarity matters", Psychological Bulletin: 59 studies, 238 effect sizes; overall interleaving g = 0.42. Largest for visual materials such as paintings (g = 0.67). A small, significant effect for maths tasks. Strongly moderated by similarity between categories. — [Würzburg PDF](https://www.psychologie.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/06020400/2019/Brunmair_Richter_in_press__2019_META-ANALYSIS_OF_INTERLEAVED_LEARNING.pdf); [Frontiers corrigendum note](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02296/full)

**LLM-era studies (2024–2026)**
- **[PR] Bastani et al., PNAS 122(26), 1 Jul 2025** ("Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning"). Field experiment with ~1,000 high-school maths students. During practice, grades rose 48% (GPT Base) and 127% (GPT Tutor). On the later exam without AI, GPT Base students did 17% worse than the control group. GPT Tutor (prompted to give hints, not answers) "largely mitigated" the harm but did not beat the control group. Mechanism: students used GPT-4 "as a crutch". — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4895486); [Wharton coverage](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education); [RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nas/journl/v122y2025pe2422633122.html)
- **[PR] Melumad & Yun, PNAS Nexus 4 (2025) pgaf316**: 7 experiments, n = 10,462. People who learned from LLM syntheses developed shallower knowledge than web-search users, even with identical facts and even when LLM answers had live links. They spent less effort and wrote shorter, less fact-rich, more homogeneous advice. 1,501 blind raters judged their advice less helpful and trustworthy. Proposed mechanism: summaries remove the learner's own discovery and synthesis. Caveat: part of the depth measure is self-reported. — [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/learning-from-ai-summaries-leads-to-shallower-knowledge-than-web-search/); [InfoDocket](https://www.infodocket.com/2025/10/28/new-pnas-nexus-journal-article-ai-produces-shallower-knowledge-than-web-search/)
- **[PR] Stadler, Bannert & Sailer, Computers in Human Behavior 160:108386 (2024)**: 91 university students, randomly assigned ChatGPT-3.5 vs Google for a socio-scientific inquiry. The ChatGPT group reported significantly lower mental effort but produced weaker reasoning and argumentation. — [Augsburg repository](https://opus.bibliothek.uni-augsburg.de/opus4/frontdoor/index/index/docId/114673); [TUM portal](https://portal.fis.tum.de/en/publications/cognitive-ease-at-a-cost-llms-reduce-mental-effort-but-compromise/)
- **[PR] Fan et al., British Journal of Educational Technology 56(2):489–530 (2024/2025)**, "Beware of metacognitive laziness". The ChatGPT group improved essay scores most, but knowledge gain and transfer did not differ from other support conditions. Process logs showed less metacognitive engagement: learners looped back to ChatGPT instead of reflecting. — [arXiv 2412.09315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09315v1)
- **[PP] Kosmyna et al. (MIT Media Lab), "Your Brain on ChatGPT"**, arXiv 2506.08872 (June 2025, revised Dec 2025): 54 participants (18 in session 4). The LLM-essay group showed weaker recall, including of their own essays, lower neural engagement and more homogeneous language ("cognitive debt"). Participants who wrote unaided first and then used an LLM showed higher recall. Small sample, not peer-reviewed, methods disputed. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08872v1); [MIT Media Lab](https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/your-brain-on-chatgpt/)
- **[PP] An et al. (July 2025)**: LLM-generated multiple-choice retrieval-practice questions in two data-science courses (~60 students). 89% accuracy vs 73% in a week without such practice. Not randomised by week. Instructors still had to "manually verify and revise" questions. — [arXiv 2507.05629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.05629v2)
- **[PP, AIME-Con 2026] Leong et al.**: LLM-generated retrieval-practice items had weaker psychometric properties than human-written ones in an intro psychology course, so the authors recommend humans in the loop. — [ACL Anthology 2026.aimecon-main.21](https://aclanthology.org/2026.aimecon-main.21.pdf)

**Generating review prompts with LLMs**
- **[NOTE] Andy Matuschak (2023 working notes).** LLMs reliably generate "shallow variations" of existing prompt questions, which is easier than generating good Q/A pairs. Results are "much better" when the learner adds "a word or two hinting at what aspect… I want to reinforce" (e.g. scribbling "why" over a highlight). Giving GPT-4 the community's prompt-writing principles "seems to" help, inconsistently. "Writing good spaced repetition memory prompts is hard." — [Matuschak: shallow variations](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zEhne31FD53eNQw3bpcuomfxMYL3s1qkhbF); [extra hints about angle](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zomoPzCNzSi5GqtfTeVWgm7RjmiArjS8vvM5); [prompt-writing principles](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zrqgkr9n3eCMNsAPDsRozt3HLd8nRT5nVASc); [writing good prompts is hard](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/Writing_good_spaced_repetition_memory_prompts_is_hard)
- A January 2024 blog comparison of GPT-4 Turbo, GPT-3.5 and local LLMs for Anki flashcards on a maths topic rated the cards on truthfulness, self-containment and atomicity. The author warns the results date quickly [blog]. — [R-bloggers](https://www.r-bloggers.com/2024/01/comparing-gpt-4-3-5-and-some-offline-local-llms-at-the-task-of-generating-flashcards-for-spaced-repetition-e-g-anki/)

### Inferences
- Reading an AI-written topic page is close to "rereading" or someone else's "summarization", both rated low utility, and it gives up the generation effect (~0.4 SD). The page's learning value comes from what the user does with it: answering questions from memory, explaining "why", and comparing related topics.
- Learning features that follow from the strongest evidence:
  1. **Agent-led retrieval quiz from a page** (recall-type questions first, then feedback against the page). Feedback roughly doubles the testing effect in Rowland.
  2. **Spaced re-visits** of topic pages, with intervals growing over time (Cepeda).
  3. **"Why/how" questions** per page (elaborative interrogation / self-explanation, moderate utility).
  4. **Mixed quizzes across linked, similar topics** (interleaving helps most when categories are similar and easy to confuse). Explicit links between related topics make this possible.
- Bastani's GPT Tutor result suggests a guardrail: in review mode the agent should not show the answer or page section before the user has attempted recall. Hints first, answer after the attempt.
- The user's goal ("build on what I already know") is best served by a pre-session retrieval step ("what do you remember about X?") before the agent shows the page. This combines retrieval practice with gap detection, because whatever the user cannot recall becomes the gap list.
- Matuschak's notes and Leong et al. suggest LLM-generated questions need a target angle and human curation. Prompts should come from the user's stated open questions or emphasised points, not from a blanket "make flashcards from this page".

### Gaps
- No controlled study was found on learning from a personal AI-maintained wiki specifically, or on adults reviewing technical topics with an agent quiz.
- Exact Roediger & Karpicke percentages, the Brunmair & Richter maths/words moderator values and Cepeda's pooled effect were not verified from the primary text.
- Evidence on LLM-generated flashcards (as opposed to MCQs) improving learning outcomes is absent. Only quality ratings and small MCQ studies exist.
- The long-term effect of the "write first, then use LLM" ordering (Kosmyna session 4) rests on 18 participants.

## Q4. Existing review / spaced-repetition tools for Obsidian and Markdown, and how they mark cards

### Takeaway
The dominant Obsidian plugin (Spaced Repetition by st3v3nmw, ~2.6k GitHub stars) marks cards inline with `::` / `:::` / `?` / `??` separators and cloze via `==highlight==`, `**bold**` or `{{…}}`. It groups decks by `#flashcards/...` tags and can schedule whole notes for review via `#review`. Anki bridges (Obsidian_to_Anki) wrap cards in `START`/`END` blocks. Several plugins use OpenAI to generate cards. Others schedule whole notes by frontmatter properties or keep scheduling outside the notes in JSON. This last approach matters for a git-tracked, CLI-validated vault.

### Cited Findings
- **Obsidian Spaced Repetition (st3v3nmw).** Single-line `Question::Answer`; reversed `Question:::Answer`; multi-line with a `?` line; reversed multi-line with `??`. Cloze via `==text==`, `**bold**`, `{{curly}}` or custom patterns. Decks via `#flashcards` or `#flashcards/SUB_DECK`. Note-level review via the `#review` tag and "Review note as …" commands. Scheduler listed as FSRS or SM-2 (sources differ on whether FSRS has shipped yet). 2.6k stars, 312 forks [DOC]. — [GitHub repo](https://github.com/st3v3nmw/obsidian-spaced-repetition)
- The same plugin stores card scheduling data in HTML comments next to cards and note scheduling in YAML frontmatter, so the plugin writes into note files [SEC; from search summary, not confirmed on the docs page, which returned 404]. — [Algolia docsearch index](https://docsearch.algolia.com/mcp/docs/repo/st3v3nmw/obsidian-spaced-repetition)
- **Obsidian_to_Anki.** Cards sit between `START` and `END` lines with a note-type line ("Basic" etc.). The parser reads only text between the markers. Requires AnkiConnect with `app://obsidian.md` in its CORS list. — [GitHub (ObsidianToAnki)](https://github.com/ObsidianToAnki/Obsidian_to_Anki); [Obsidian forum thread](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/obsidian-to-anki-v3-4-0-a-feature-rich-plugin-that-allows-you-to-add-notes-from-obsidian-to-anki/5030)
- A May 2025 roundup lists 24 plugins:
  - AI generation: AI-AnkiSync (optional OpenAI), Auto Anki (GPT), text2anki-openai (cards from bullet points), Spaced Repetition AI (FSRS + optional OpenAI).
  - Whole-note review: Review (into daily notes), Repeat (a `repeat` frontmatter property; can hide notes until due), The Queue (frontmatter templates), Spaced Everything (SM-2), Incremental Writing.
  - Non-intrusive tracking: Recall, which "tracks notes without editing them" and keeps the schedule in external JSON (Anki/SM-2/Leitner).
  — [obsidianstats roundup](https://www.obsidianstats.com/posts/2025-05-01-spaced-repetition-plugins)
- A separate FSRS community plugin exists for FSRS-based scheduling. — [Obsidian community: fsrs](https://community.obsidian.md/plugins/fsrs)

### Inferences
- ms-kb writes only through the CLI and commits every write. A plugin that writes scheduling data into notes (HTML comments or frontmatter on each review) would create many small commits, or "outside edits" that `commit_external` has to absorb. A non-intrusive design (schedule kept outside the vault, or in ms-kb's own state file next to the config, like `vault-<kb>.json`) avoids churn. The Recall plugin shows this design exists.
- For compatibility, any review questions ms-kb stores in a page could use the st3v3nmw `Question::Answer` or `?` syntax inside a dedicated section (e.g. "## Review questions"). Users could then optionally review them with the plugin, while the agent can still quiz from the same section.
- The user did not ask for per-day history. Note-level review (when a topic page was last reviewed and when it is next due) is closer to the user's intent than card-level SRS, and fits the "Repeat"/`#review` model.

### Gaps
- No usage data or outcome evidence for any of these plugins was found. Download counts were not retrieved.
- The exact current storage format (`<!--SR:...-->` comment, `sr-due`/`sr-interval`/`sr-ease` frontmatter keys) of st3v3nmw's plugin was not confirmed from its docs (404). Verify before relying on it.

## Q5. Keeping a topic page concise while accumulating knowledge: evergreen notes, progressive summarisation, MOCs, granularity

### Takeaway
Matuschak's evergreen notes favour atomic, concept-oriented, densely linked notes that are refined over time. Forte's progressive summarisation favours layered, opportunistic, just-in-time compression and warns that "more summarization is not better". No evidence-based granularity rule for "one page per topic" was found. The practitioner consensus is a compact concept/topic page plus explicit links and an index, with length targets and periodic normalisation enforced by a style guide.

### Cited Findings
- Matuschak: evergreen notes are "written and organized to evolve, contribute, and accumulate over time, across projects". Principles: atomic, concept-oriented, densely linked, associative over hierarchical, written for yourself. "'Better note-taking' misses the point; what matters is 'better thinking'." [NOTE] — [Matuschak, Evergreen notes](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/Evergreen_notes)
- Forte's progressive summarisation layers: L1 original notes → L2 bold key passages → L3 highlight within bold → L4 an executive summary in your own words (optional L5 remix). — [dsebastien concept note](https://concepts.dsebastien.net/concept/progressive-summarization/); [Forte Labs series](https://fortelabs.com/series/ps/)
- Forte: "It is absolutely NOT the goal to stuff every single note through all the layers… More summarization is not better." Summarising should be opportunistic ("when I'm already reviewing the note for some other purpose") and just in time, done when working on a project or problem. — [Forte, Progressive Summarization III](https://fortelabs.com/progressive-summarization-iii-guidelines-and-principles-1466e29fea80); [Progressive Summarization II](https://fortelabs.com/blog/progressive-summarization-ii-examples-and-metaphors/)
- LLM-wiki practitioners: set length targets and page templates in the style guide, normalise every 20–30 sources, and keep a master index with one line per page [PRAC]. — [Ganglani](https://www.kunalganglani.com/blog/llm-wiki-karpathy-local-knowledge-base). Karpathy's index.md is a "content-oriented catalog" with one-line summaries. — [Karpathy gist](https://gist.github.com/karpathy/442a6bf555914893e9891c11519de94f). Sayfun caps the word count of the map file ("map, not a journal") [SEC]. — [Sayfun](https://omiid.me/notebook/66/i-read-all-1-061-comments-on-karpathy-s-llm-wiki-gist)
- Six-month first-hand report (35 pages, mostly Claude-edited): each new article makes Claude touch 8–12 existing pages, including adding backlinks, updating the concepts index, flagging a contradiction and refining a TL;DR [PRAC; from search summary, not fetched]. — [openaitoolshub, "Six Months In"](https://www.openaitoolshub.org/en/blog/karpathy-llm-wiki)

### Inferences
- The user's design (one consolidated page per topic, mainly one page changed per session, targeted link edits on neighbours) sits between Matuschak's atomic notes and Karpathy's topic/entity pages. Two ideas from the sources keep it concise:
  1. A short top summary ("TL;DR" / Forte layer-4 style) followed by sections.
  2. Detail that grows beyond the page's scope gets split into a linked sub-topic page rather than appended. Atomicity applies at the sub-topic level.
- Forte's "more summarization is not better" and the Broken Telephone drift result together argue against re-compressing a page on every session. Rewrite when a page is used and the user is present (opportunistic), and keep details that were removed recoverable through git.
- A Map-of-Content or index page per area can make links between topics explicit without over-linking inside topic pages. Ganglani's "link first mention only" rule limits clutter.

### Gaps
- No empirical study was found on optimal note granularity, or on topic pages vs atomic notes for learning or retrieval.
- Maps of Content (Nick Milo / Linking Your Thinking) were not sourced in this pass, so there is no citation for MOC specifics.
- Matuschak's notes on titles-as-APIs and on note size were not retrieved.
